GAME DESIGN
1. Game Overview
a. Title: Orasyon: Spirits of the Archipelago
b. Genre: 2D Word Puzzle / Turn-Based Battle Game
c. Platform: PC (Windows)
d. Target Audience: Casual puzzle players, students, and players interested in Philippine mythology
and folklore.
e. Game Summary: Orasyon: Spirits of the Archipelago is a word-puzzle battle game inspired by
Bookworm Adventures. Players form words from a letter grid to cast attacks against creatures from
Philippine mythology. Each creature has a unique mechanic that changes the letter grid, creating
different challenges while keeping the core gameplay simple and focused.
f. Similar Games: Bookworm Adventures, Boggle, Wordscapes, and other word-forming puzzle
games.
g. Key Selling Points: Word-based combat, Philippine mythology theme, unique creature mechanics
that directly affect the letter grid, simple controls, and a focused scope suitable for a studentdeveloped game.
2. Core Gameplay/Mechanics
a. Core Loop: Start battle > Observe the letter grid > Select connected letters to form a word >
Validate the word > Deal damage or activate an effect > Enemy takes a turn > Enemy attacks or
changes the grid > Repeat until the player or enemy is defeated.
b. Objective & Goals: Defeat mythological creatures by forming valid and strategically useful words,
survive enemy attacks, adapt to each creature's special mechanic, and complete all chapters leading
to the final encounter.
c. Player Action: The player selects adjacent letter tiles to form a word, submits the word, and uses
the resulting attack against the enemy. Longer words generally produce stronger attacks,
encouraging players to balance quick attacks with more powerful combinations.
d. Key Mechanics: Letter-grid word formation, local word validation using an English dictionary,
word-length-based damage, player and enemy HP, special tiles, turn-based enemy actions, and
creature-specific grid disruption. Filipino/Tagalog word support may be considered as a future
enhancement rather than a core requirement.
e. Game Progression: The game is divided into chapters. Each chapter introduces a mythological
creature and its main mechanic. Regular encounters allow players to learn the mechanic, while boss
encounters provide a stronger version of the challenge. Completing a chapter unlocks the next one.
f. Control Scheme: Mouse: select letter tiles, form words, submit actions, and navigate menus.
Keyboard: optional menu navigation and confirmation. The controls are designed around simple
point-and-click interaction so the player can focus on word formation.
3. Narrative
a. Story Summary: The player takes the role of a young albularyo who carries an old book of orasyon.
As supernatural creatures begin appearing across the land, the player travels through different
locations and uses words of power to confront them. The goal is to overcome each guardian and
eventually face the Bakunawa.
b. World-Building: The game is set in a stylized version of the Philippine archipelago inspired by local
folklore. Each chapter represents a different environment associated with a mythological creature,
such as a backyard mound, a balete tree, village rooftops, a deep forest, and a cosmic ocean.
c. Plot Structure: The story follows a simple chapter-based structure: introduction to the player's role
> encounter with a creature > progression through the creature's environment > boss battle > next
chapter. The final chapter leads to the confrontation with the Bakunawa.
d. Optional Narrative: Narrative is kept lightweight to avoid scope creep. Short dialogue boxes,
chapter introductions, and brief creature descriptions may be used to provide context without
requiring long cutscenes or extensive voice acting.
e. Key Events: The player receives the book of orasyon; the first encounter with a Duwende
introduces word combat; later chapters introduce the Kapre, Manananggal, and Tiyanak with
increasingly disruptive mechanics; the player reaches the final confrontation with the Bakunawa
during an eclipse.
f. Characters: Player Character – a young albularyo who uses the book of orasyon. Duwende – an
unpredictable trickster that disrupts the letter grid. Kapre – a powerful tree-dwelling spirit that
obscures tiles with smoke. Manananggal – a dangerous creature that changes its defensive state.
Tiyanak – a deceptive forest creature that scrambles the grid. Bakunawa – the final cosmic serpent
and strongest encounter.
g. Tone and Themes Mystical, adventurous, slightly eerie, and accessible rather than horror-focused.
The game emphasizes Filipino folklore, resourcefulness, word knowledge, and overcoming
supernatural challenges.
4. Mechanical and System
a. Combat System: Combat is turn-based and centered on word formation. The player selects a
sequence of adjacent letters to create a valid word. The word is then converted into an attack, with
longer valid words generally dealing more damage. After the player's turn, the enemy performs an
attack or special action.
b. Abilities: Abilities are represented by special tiles and word effects. Special tiles may provide
bonus damage, healing, protection, or other simple effects. A limited set of effects will be used in the
initial version to keep combat readable and manageable.
c. Economy: The core game does not require a monetary economy. Optional rewards such as points,
stars, or chapter ratings may be awarded for successful battles and can be used to track performance
rather than purchase gameplay advantages.
d. Progression System: Progression is chapter-based. Players unlock new encounters by defeating
previous enemies. Difficulty increases through enemy health, damage, board disruption, and more
demanding puzzle conditions rather than through a large equipment or inventory system.
e. Interaction System: Players interact with letter tiles, battle buttons, menus, chapter selection, and
simple dialogue panels. Letter tiles provide immediate visual and audio feedback when selected,
submitted, or rejected.
f. AI & Behavior: Enemies use simple rule-based behavior rather than complex navigation AI. Each
creature has a predictable attack pattern and a signature grid mechanic. For example, Duwende can
remove valuable tiles, Kapre can hide several tiles with smoke, Tiyanak can scramble the grid, and
Bakunawa can gradually consume tiles from the board.
5. Visual and Audio Design
a. Art Style: Stylized 2D digital illustration with a colorful fantasy-folklore presentation. The visual
style should be approachable and game-like while retaining recognizable elements of Philippine
mythology.
b. Audio: Atmospheric background music inspired by Philippine settings and fantasy adventure.
Sound effects include tile selection, word submission, successful attacks, damage, special mechanics,
victory, and defeat.
c. Color Palette: Each chapter uses a distinct environment palette while maintaining readable highcontrast letter tiles and battle UI. Mystical effects use brighter accent colors to distinguish attacks
and special mechanics from the background.
d. UI/UX: The interface prioritizes the letter grid, player HP, enemy HP, current word, turn indicator,
and available effects. Buttons and tiles should be large enough for comfortable mouse interaction,
with clear feedback for valid and invalid words.
e. Character & Asset Design: The player character and mythological creatures use simplified 2D
designs that are practical for a student project. Each major creature requires an idle state, attack
state, and damage state. Environments can use static backgrounds with limited effects to reduce
animation workload.
6. Level Design
a. Level Layout: Each level is a single battle-focused screen. The enemy occupies one side of the
screen while the player interface and letter grid occupy the main interaction area. Chapters contain a
sequence of encounters followed by a stronger boss battle.
b. Environment Types: Backyard or mound for Duwende, balete tree for Kapre, village rooftops for
Manananggal, deep forest for Tiyanak, and a cosmic ocean or eclipse setting for Bakunawa.
c. Pacing: Early encounters are short and introduce the basic word-battle rules. Later encounters
gradually require more planning because enemies disrupt the board. Boss fights are longer and
combine higher enemy strength with their signature mechanics.
d. Environmental Storytelling: Backgrounds, creature designs, chapter names, visual effects, and
short descriptions communicate the setting and folklore without requiring large maps or extensive
dialogue.
e. Key Challenges: Finding useful words under limited board conditions, deciding when to use long
words, responding to enemy grid disruption, maintaining enough HP to survive longer battles, and
adapting to each creature's unique mechanic.
f. Difficulty & Progression: Difficulty increases gradually through stronger enemy statistics, more
disruptive mechanics, and less predictable board conditions. Each new creature introduces one
major complication before later encounters combine challenge types.
7. Monetization
a. Business Model: The game is not monetized and is intended as a student-developed project.
b. Premium Features: None. All planned gameplay content is included in the base game.
c. AIP/ADS placements: None. The game does not use advertisements or in-app purchases.
8. Technical Requirement
a. Engine: Unity with C#.
b. Performance Goals Target approximately 60 FPS on the intended Windows PC hardware, with
responsive letter selection, word validation, and battle animations. The game should avoid
unnecessary loading between individual encounters.
c. Platform-Specific Features: The initial target is Windows PC with mouse and keyboard support. The
game uses Unity UI and 2D systems and does not require an online connection for the core
gameplay.
d. Multiplayer Requirements: None. The game is designed as a single-player experience to keep
development scope focused.
9. Production Plan
a. Key Milestone: Gameplay concept and design completion > word-grid prototype > functional word
validation and combat > first complete creature encounter > enemy-specific mechanics > chapter
and boss implementation > art/audio integration > internal testing and balancing > final build.
b. Team Roles: Game Programmer – implements the letter grid, word validation, combat, enemy
behavior, and game flow. Game Designer/Project Manager – defines mechanics, balances difficulty,
manages documentation, and coordinates development. 2D Artist – creates characters, creatures,
backgrounds, UI assets, and effects. Audio/QA Support – prepares sound assets, performs
playtesting, and documents bugs and balancing issues.
c. Cost & Budget: Low-cost student project. Development will prioritize free or already available tools
and assets. Any optional spending should be limited to required art, audio, or Unity assets.
d. Development Timeline: An indicative 15-week schedule is provided below.
e. Risk and Mitigation: Risk of scope creep will be reduced by limiting the game to a small number of
chapters and mechanics. Development delays will be mitigated by prioritizing the core word-battle
loop before optional content. Art and animation workload will be reduced through static
backgrounds and limited creature animations. Dictionary and word-validation issues will be tested
early with a fixed local word list. Difficulty and balance problems will be addressed through repeated
internal playtesting.
